## Context

BU's Office of Student Programs and Leadership runs a Peer Mentor program that pairs small groups of incoming first-year students with an older student who co-teaches a weekly seminar through the fall semester. The job is less about delivering content and more about being a steady, useful presence while first-years figure out how college actually works.

## Contribution

Co-taught 14 weekly seminars for a small cohort of 11 first-year students, with topics ranging from academic planning and time management to navigating campus resources and getting involved. Worked alongside a BU staff instructor to refine activities, address student needs, and support successful college transitions.

## Approach

Treated the seminar as a space for guided practice rather than a lecture. Made space for one-on-one conversations with students who needed them, and used the weekly rhythm to build a small, durable community within the cohort.

## Outcome and learning

The strongest takeaway was the difference between being available and being useful. The students who got the most out of the program weren't the ones I talked the most at — they were the ones I made it easy to talk to when they were ready.
